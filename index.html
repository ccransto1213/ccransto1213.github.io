My thesis developed exact asymptotics for the spectra of neural tangent and conjugate kernels: deterministic equivalents, Stieltjes transform methods, and the phase behavior that governs when a model can recover signal from noise. The practical payoff is that generalization error becomes a computable function of the spectrum rather than something you can only measure after the fact.

I now build production machine learning systems at [Company], where the same instincts apply under less forgiving constraints — auditability, determinism, and the requirement that a model's failure modes be known in advance rather than discovered in review. Research

Let a kernel matrix be built from a network with p parameters and n samples, and let both grow with their ratio fixed. The empirical eigenvalue distribution converges to a deterministic law, and once that law is known, training and test risk, the effect of ridge regularization, and the value of additional data all follow from it. The interesting behavior sits at the spectral edge: signal appears as eigenvalues that detach from the bulk, but only past a threshold, below which the signal is invisible regardless of sample size.

  • Deterministic equivalents. Hamilton–Jacobi characterizations of the resolvent, and GCV-based risk estimation in the proportional regime.
  • Scaling laws. Finite-width corrections to NTK scaling under dual power-law covariance; where the predicted exponents break.
  • Spiked models. BBP transitions and signal detectability for kernels that are neither Gaussian nor infinitely wide.
